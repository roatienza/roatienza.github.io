1 · The phrase, and the words

No word is ever spoken alone. Each one is rendered as a sentence:

Please say {word} again.      →   "Please say receipt again."

A bare word is not a fair test. Measured against the live API: Receipt. comes back as Recess or Receipts, while Please say receipt again. comes back exactly, every time. The carrier gives the ASR a sentence to fall into — and it is how the word is spoken in use.

3 · What Wilson 95% is

5,619 of 7,365 cases passed: 76.3%. That is a sample. Re-run with different seeds and it would not land on 76.3% again. The Wilson score interval is the range of true rates that would produce a sample like this 95 times out of 100.

p  = k / n
d  = 1 + z²/n
c  = p + z²/(2n)
s  = z · sqrt( p(1−p)/n + z²/(4n²) )
lo = (c − s) / d        hi = (c + s) / d        z = 1.96

Worked example — the core band

k = 1,214, n = 1,245, z = 1.96
p = 0.9751
d = 1 + 3.8416/1245         = 1.003086
c = 0.9751 + 3.8416/2490    = 0.976643
s = 1.96 · sqrt(0.9751·0.0249/1245 + 3.8416/(4·1245²))
  = 1.96 · 0.004486         = 0.008792
lo = (0.976643 − 0.008792)/1.003086 = 0.9649   → 96.5%
hi = (0.976643 + 0.008792)/1.003086 = 0.9824   → 98.2%

Read it as: the voice is reliable on at least 96.5% of core-word cases, with a point estimate of 97.5%. Two voices whose intervals overlap are not distinguishable by this run.

Why not p ± 1.96·sqrt(p(1−p)/n)

The normal approximation fails exactly where this evaluation lives — in the small cells. It gives 0 of 10 an interval of zero width, claims certainty from ten trials, and runs past 100% for 24 of 25. Wilson is not symmetric, never leaves [0,1], and stays honest at small n.

4 · The WER of this evaluation, and where it assumes the ASR is accurate

WER is (S + D + I) / N — substitutions, deletions and insertions over the reference word count. Here the reference is the carrier sentence itself (please say WORD again, 4 words) and the hypothesis is what the ASR wrote. That makes every error an ASR-mediated number: the transcript is treated as ground truth about what was said, so a homophone the voice said correctly is charged to the voice.

measurevaluewhat it measures
raw WER8.19% 1,811 S + 1 D + 603 I over 29,490 reference words. The benchmark number.
carrier WER0.16% edits on please / say / again only (29 deletions, 7 insertions in 22,095 words). The frame almost never breaks — so nearly every raw-WER edit sits in the word slot.
target WER33.05% edits inside the word slot only: 1,782 S + 1 D + 661 I over 7,395 target words. This is the honest per-word error rate of the pipeline (voice + ASR together).
adjusted target WER31.95% 85 homophone substitutions re-scored as correct — a listener would have understood the word. The 1.10-point gap is the homophone share of the assumption.

Where the assumption bites. The raw WER of 8.19% looks close to the pass-rate complement (23.7%) only because the carrier dilutes it. On the word slot alone the pipeline "errors" 33% of the time — but the field band contributes 2,414 of those edits, and there the heard forms (anaesthesia → anistis now, amoxicillin → aoxxicill in) are ASR garble of long jargon, not mispronunciations. WER here measures the ASR as much as the voice. The carrier WER of 0.16% is the proof: when the words are easy, this same pipeline transcribes this same voice almost perfectly.

The confusion pairs, counted

Every failing case contributes one (expected → heard) pair. The homophone-marked rows are pairs where the two forms sound identical — the voice said the word, the ASR spelled it differently. Everything else is a real mishearing or a real mispronunciation; play the clip to tell which.
