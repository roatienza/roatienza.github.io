Common words — sample evaluations

How many common English words can a voice say correctly? Every word is spoken inside one fixed sentence, transcribed by an ASR, and scored. The numbers below are samples, so each one carries a Wilson 95% interval.

What this page is. A worked demo of the evaluation in apps/yoob-tts-web/wordlist/ of Tap-Mobile/tts-tools-generic. Voice w1243, seed 7, ASR yoob-asr-2, run 2026-11-02. The full write-up is tests/WORDLIST_EVAL_REPORT.md.

1 · The phrase, and the words

No word is ever spoken alone. Each one is rendered as a sentence:

Please say {word} again.      →   "Please say receipt again."

A bare word is not a fair test. Measured against the live API: Receipt. comes back as Recess or Receipts, while Please say receipt again. comes back exactly, every time. The carrier gives the ASR a sentence to fall into — and it is how the word is spoken in use.

2 · What Wilson 95% is

389 of 415 words passed: 93.7%. That is a sample. Re-run on a different draw of words, or a different seed, and it would not land on 93.7% again. The Wilson score interval is the range of true rates that would produce a sample like this 95 times out of 100.

p  = k / n
d  = 1 + z²/n
c  = p + z²/(2n)
s  = z · sqrt( p(1−p)/n + z²/(4n²) )
lo = (c − s) / d        hi = (c + s) / d        z = 1.96

Worked example — the core band

k = 389, n = 415, z = 1.96
p = 0.9373
d = 1 + 3.8416/415          = 1.00926
c = 0.9373 + 3.8416/830     = 0.94193
s = 1.96 · sqrt(0.9373·0.0627/415 + 3.8416/(4·415²))
  = 1.96 · 0.012134         = 0.023783
lo = (0.94193 − 0.023783)/1.00926 = 0.9098   → 91.0%
hi = (0.94193 + 0.023783)/1.00926 = 0.9569   → 95.7%

Read it as: the voice is reliable on at least 91% of core words, with a point estimate of 93.7%. Two voices whose intervals overlap are not distinguishable by this run.

Why not p ± 1.96·sqrt(p(1−p)/n)

The normal approximation fails exactly where this evaluation lives — in the small cells. It gives 0 of 10 an interval of zero width, claims certainty from ten trials, and runs past 100% for 24 of 25. Wilson is not symmetric, never leaves [0,1], and stays honest at small n.

3 · Where WER assumes the ASR is accurate

WER is (S + D + I) / N — substitutions, deletions and insertions over the reference word count. It compares a reference transcript to a hypothesis transcript. In a TTS evaluation the hypothesis comes from an ASR, and that is where the assumption enters:

WER treats the ASR transcript as ground truth about what the voice said. Every substitution is charged to the TTS. The ASR is assumed to have heard correctly. This evaluation does not use WER — it uses a per-word pass rate — but the same assumption sits underneath it, and here is exactly where it breaks.

what the ASR doesWER charges it towho is actually at fault
writes a homophone (their → there)the TTS nobody — the voice was right
drops a short function word (a → nothing)the TTS usually the ASR
returns HTTP 400 (diagnosis)the TTS, as 100% error the pipeline
mis-hears a rare word (prognosis → prague)the TTS the ASR, unless the audio is checked
is deterministic — six passes, one answer— repeats cannot average any of the above away

The size of the assumption, measured

22 of the 26 core failures are homophones. The voice said the word; the scorer compares strings, not sounds, and counted a failure. Re-scoring those 22 as passes:

The rule this demo follows: read the transcript before believing a failure. A failure whose heard field is a homophone of expected is not evidence against the voice.

5 · Reproducing

cd apps/yoob-tts-web/wordlist

python3 build_wordlist.py --out words_v1.jsonl          # frozen list, hash-checked
python3 run_words.py --list words_v1.jsonl --band core \
        --voices w1243 --seeds 7                        # the run above
python3 report.py core_w1243.jsonl --csv core_w1243.csv # the report

Cost: one word-voice-seed is one TTS render (~0.3 s of audio) plus one ASR pass (~1 s wall). 2,455 words × 34 voices × 3 seeds is 204,000 pairs, roughly 60–85 hours serial; shard 8–16 ways.

6 · Known limits

  • The list is hand-kept, not corpus-derived. It is stable, not authoritative.
  • One voice, one seed, in the run shown. The seed swings a single voice by up to 3 words out of 10 on the hard panel (h_brit_m: 6/10 at seed 7, 3/10 at seed 8), so one seed per word is not a measurement — it is one sample.
  • The scorer compares strings. Homophones fail. Section 3 is the correction; it is applied by hand here and not by the harness.
  • Three expected fields (it, are, nose) are unmatchable as written, and are counted as failures against a voice that said the word.
  • A 400 from the transcriber is an error, not a failure, and is left out of the denominator.
