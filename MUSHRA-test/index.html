How to score (MUSHRA)

This is a MUSHRA test (MUltiple Stimuli with Hidden Reference and Anchor, ITU-R BS.1534) — the standard method for comparing high-quality audio systems. For each sentence you will hear 10 anonymized stimuli (labeled Stimulus A–J). Among them, hidden at random positions, are:

  • 🎯 the original human recording (the hidden reference),
  • 🔇 two bandwidth-limited anchors (muffled versions of it),
  • 🔊 and 8 versions of the same sentence — 7 TTS systems plus one open copy of the reference.

Rate every stimulus on a continuous 0–100 scale relative to the Reference, which you can open at any time (button below the grid):

80–100Excellent — indistinguishable from the reference. 60–80Good — clearly lower quality, but not annoying. 40–60Fair — lower quality; somewhat annoying. 20–40Poor — annoying; clearly degraded. 0–20Bad — very annoying; barely listenable.
  • Use headphones in a quiet room if possible.
  • Play each stimulus (at least once) before rating it — the slider unlocks after playback starts.
  • Loop is on by default — samples repeat until you pause, which makes small differences easier to hear. You may toggle it per stimulus.
  • Compare freely: unlike a 1–5 rating, you can go back and forth between stimuli before deciding. The reference defines 100; the muffled anchors belong near the bottom.
  • Rate what you hear — the identities are hidden on purpose.
  • Rate all 10 stimuli of a sentence, then move to the next. After all 5 sentences press Submit ratings. Scores are saved anonymously; no personal data is collected.
